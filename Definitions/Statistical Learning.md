## Plain English Definition
Statistical learning is the broad set of methods used to understand data. In ISLP, it includes both supervised problems, where we predict an output from inputs, and unsupervised problems, where we search for structure without an output.

## Formal Definition
$$
\text{Statistical learning} = \{\text{supervised learning},\ \text{unsupervised learning}\}
$$
| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $\text{supervised learning}$ | Learning with inputs and an observed response |
| $\text{unsupervised learning}$ | Learning from inputs alone, without a response |

## Intuitive Analogy
Statistical learning is like a toolkit for making sense of a messy workshop. Sometimes you already know what object you are trying to build, so you use the tools to shape raw material toward that target. Other times, you do not know what is there yet, so you sort, inspect, and organize the pieces until patterns emerge.

## When to Use It
- ✅ When you want a general framework for prediction, explanation, or structure discovery from data.

## When It Breaks Down
- ❌ When the data are too poor, inconsistent, or irrelevant to support any meaningful pattern-finding.
